SableEdit demo

Click any shape to edit it. Click empty space to deselect. Every shape here is a live element; edits write straight to its attributes. Drag anywhere on the selected shape to move it, or click it again without dragging to switch mode.
mode: -tool: pointernothing selected
  • Tools & context menu: right-click (or Ctrl-click on a Mac) anywhere on the canvas. Use Once arms a tool for a single shape and then returns to the pointer; Switch Tool keeps it until you pick Pointer again (or press Esc). Circle: press for the centre, drag out to the radius, release. Rectangle: press one corner, release on the opposite one (any direction). Ellipse: the same two corners, of its bounding box. Line: press the start, release at the end. Esc during a drag cancels it. Shapes you draw land in the free-drawing area at the bottom and are editable like any other; creating one is a single undo step.
  • Permissions: the last two shapes in the grid are held back by data-sable-policy attributes in the markup (view source; the script only says policy: [{ select: '.edit', can: 'all' }, 'markup']). The path can be reshaped and have interior nodes added or deleted, but its two endpoints are pinned (grey; they can't be dragged or deleted), so it only offers edit mode: no move, scale or rotate. Whatever you drag in it snaps to the 10-unit grid drawn behind it (snap: 10): a node and the bezier handles that travel with it snap together, so the curve keeps its shape. Its stroke can't be changed (Set stroke is refused) but its fill can. The polygon can be moved, scaled, rotated and have its vertices dragged, but double-clicking never adds or deletes a vertex; it can't leave its cell (bounds: a dragged vertex or a move stops at the wall, a scale or rotation that would cross it is refused); and the only attribute it lets you change is stroke-width, clamped to 1–6 (ranges), so Set fill and Set stroke are refused but Stroke width 20 gives 6. The readout says what was refused.
  • Path: drag square nodes and round bezier handles. Hold Shift on a cubic handle to mirror its partner. Double-click the path to add a node; double-click a node to delete it. Arcs have their own handles (below).
  • Arcs (A segments in a path): click an arc's end node to show its ellipse (dashed) and handles. The two squares are the start and end nodes. The dots each do one job: the one on the dotted x-axis sets rx, the one on the dotted y-axis sets ry (hold Shift to keep it circular), the hollow ring opposite the x dot only rotates (Shift snaps to 15°), and the dot sitting on the arc flips it: drag it to the side, or the dashed part of the ellipse, where you want the arc.
  • Modes. Press any shape to select it; a drag on an unselected shape selects and moves it in one go. Once selected, drag anywhere on it to move it, or click or tap it without dragging to go to the next mode: scale → rotate/skew → edit (the handles show where you are). A line or unfilled path is grabbed by its stroke. In path and polygon edit mode, clicking the stroke waits a moment for a possible double-click (add a node) before switching mode. Shapes with no editor of their own, like text, only cycle the first two. Your last mode is kept when you select another shape.
  • Scale: the dashed box is the shape's own bounding box, drawn in the shape's own (possibly rotated) frame. Edge handles resize along their arrow, with the opposite edge held fixed; corner handles resize proportionally along the diagonal. Dragging past the opposite side flips the shape. Written to transform.
  • Rotate / skew: the bent arrows at the corners rotate about the box centre (Shift snaps to 15°). The straight arrows at the edge midpoints skew parallel to their edge, with the opposite edge held fixed. Written to transform.
  • Rect (edit): two square nodes are opposite corners (x1,y1 and x2,y2); drag one past the other and it keeps tracking. The dot on a free corner is the corner radius: it sits on the corner at radius 0 and moves into the rectangle, so its offsets are the radii (10 in, 30 down means rx=10 ry=30). Shift makes it circular. The dotted box shows the radii.
  • Circle / ellipse (edit): square handles set the radius (rx/ry for the ellipse).
  • Line (edit): drag either endpoint.
  • Polygon / polyline (edit): drag vertex squares. Double-click an edge to add a vertex, double-click a vertex to delete it (minimum 3 for a polygon, 2 for a polyline).
  • Moving rewrites rect, circle, ellipse, line, polygon, polyline and path by rewriting their own coordinates; anything else (text) gets a translate() in transform.
  • Undo/redo: Ctrl/Cmd+Z, Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z (or Ctrl+Y). A whole drag is one step.
